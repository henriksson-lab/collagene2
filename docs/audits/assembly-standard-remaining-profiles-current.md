# Remaining assembly-standard profiles

Reviewed 2026-11-28. Research only; no profile data, Rust, schemas or runtime behavior changed. No builds, tests or application/browser checks were run for this audit.

## Installed versus missing

The current `crates/assembly-standards/src/profile.rs` registry installs RFC10 part-sequence, RFC1000 Level-0 documented-body, and original MoClo2011 Level-0 two-path module grammar profiles. **YTK, EMMA, PhytoBrick and SEVA are all missing.** Generic Golden Gate execution and the installed MoClo subset do not establish their toolkit compliance.

| Requested toolkit | Independently established next scope | Remaining admission gap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| YTK 2015 | Source-part / cassette grammar with split and composite part classes | Exact complete branch/overhang table and clearly separated connector/backbone rules |
| EMMA 2017 | Mammalian modular assembly grammar | Primary detailed slot/flank data and its reuse terms not verified |
| PhytoBrick 2015 | Plant common-syntax part boundaries, including composite parts | Independently transcribe and crosscheck all 12 fusion-site labels/orientations and admissible spans; asset rights unresolved |
| Canonical SEVA | Declared vector-module boundaries and site constraints | Full circular-vector context and exact oriented flank checks; not a four-base Golden Gate slot profile |

## Primary evidence and rights

**YTK.** Lee et al. describe BsmBI source-to-part assembly, BsaI part-to-cassette assembly, and BsmBI cassette-to-multigene assembly. Figure 2 includes eight main part types, split types such as 3a/3b, and combined types such as 234. BsaI, BsmBI and NotI are required for the complete described scheme; other removed sites must not automatically become mandatory. The author-hosted paper explicitly permits article/adaptation redistribution for **noncommercial** purposes under ACS AuthorChoice. That is not permission to redistribute its figures or copied tables as MIT assets. [Author-hosted primary paper, Definition of Assembly Standard and Fig. 2](https://dueberlab.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/Pub_2015_Highly-Characterized.pdf).

The complete original supplementary table was not retrieved in this audit. In particular, do not manufacture a p4a→p4b→p5 path by assuming the intermediate `CGAC` junction rejoins the ordinary `GCTG` boundary. Require literal primary evidence for both ends of every accepted branch before installing it. Connector ordering and complete destination-vector compliance need their own explicit scope.

**PhytoBrick.** Patron et al. define 12 fusion sites and 10 syntax parts; permitted composite parts span multiple boundaries. BsaI domestication is baseline; BpiI/BsmBI removal adds compatibility with the named multigene systems. Compatible backbones also have requirements that cannot be proved from a body-only part. Figure 3 is the required exact fusion-label source. The retrieved publisher page says Free Access but does not establish a Creative Commons license; no figures or full tables are admitted for redistribution here. The published corrigendum changes an author affiliation, not the fusion-site syntax. [Primary standard, Figs. 2–3](https://nph.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/nph.13532), [corrigendum](https://nph.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/nph.13773).

**EMMA.** The authors document standardized Golden Gate mammalian parts and hierarchical multigene assembly. The accessible institutional record supplies no detailed slot table or redistribution license. The original supplementary asset and author-approved sequence/reference records remain necessary; an abstract cannot establish overhangs, split classes, flanks or mandatory forbidden sites. [Author institutional publication record, DOI 10.1021/acssynbio.7b00016](https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/emma-an-extensible-mammalian-modular-assembly-toolkit-for-the-rap/). No EMMA numeric table is admitted by this audit.

**SEVA.** The official specification gives three module pairs: origin FseI/AscI, marker SwaI/PshAI, cargo PacI/SpeI; major cargo transcription runs PacI→SpeI. Its prose abbreviates one marker site as “PshA”; the same page names PshAI in the elimination list. Verify that discrepancy against the primary vector figure/reference sequence before pinning geometry. The specification also names internal sites to remove; this is a vector/module constraint, not evidence of actual oriV, marker or host functionality. [Official structure and contribution rules](https://seva-plasmids.com/description/).

The original SEVA article is CC BY-NC 3.0, not an MIT asset source. The official database website is copyright-reserved; free material distribution is not a blanket sequence-database license. Canonical SEVA and the separately listed SEVA Golden Standard collection must not share an inferred grammar. [Primary article and rights statement](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/11473973/The_Standard_European_Vector_Architecture_SEVA_.pdf), [official collection separation](https://seva-plasmids.com/find-your-plasmid/).

## Concrete next curation

Prioritize PhytoBrick fusion syntax, then YTK only once its complete branch facts are independently verified. Both can use the existing native grammar/checker with exact profile/catalogue pins and explicit source-body coordinates; vector-only requirements remain outside that advisory scope. EMMA stays blocked on primary detailed evidence. Treat canonical SEVA as a separate vector-layout capability rather than forcing it into a part-slot checker.

Future curation should record exact source revision/locator/hash, independently authored short factual records, source rights separately from our MIT curation, literal positive/negative paths and reversed-coordinate fixtures. Do not copy publisher figures, upstream prose, application code or unlicensed datasets. No complete new factual table has been admitted here, and no toolkit-wide physical, functional or regulatory compliance is implied.
