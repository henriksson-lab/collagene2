# Benchling primer, PCR and mutagenesis interaction research

Reviewed 2026-11-25. This is public official-document and screenshot research, plus a read-only comparison with current source. No authenticated Benchling session, local application/browser check, compiler or test was run. No third-party code, prose or image assets are copied into the repository. Two official screenshots were inspected only in `/tmp`.

The useful pattern is a continuous sequence-selection → oligo editing/reuse → reconstructed product → revisioned design workflow. Implement those connections using our existing Rust algorithms. Primer3, enterprise organization, inventories, ELN integration and experimental success predictions are not prerequisites.

## Evidence and its age

**Current primer guide.** Benchling's [Use primers](https://help.benchling.com/hc/en-us/articles/39072057302541-Use-primers), updated October 2, 2025, documents selecting template bases, choosing forward/reverse, and opening a single/pair editor. The editor supports selection-based domains, explicit overhang length, cut-site additions and calculation conditions. Saved oligos can be attached after a binding search with matching/mismatch filters, linked into pairs and exported as CSV. The wizard exposes region/filter settings, uses Primer3 and lets users save selected results. These are documented interactions, not evidence that our heuristics have the same scientific capabilities.

The guide's [pair-editor screenshot](https://help.benchling.com/hc/article_attachments/39072049373453), visually inspected, shows parallel forward/reverse fields, 5′/3′ labels, 3′ coordinates and overhang lengths. Introduced and selected bases have distinct presentation. This supplies a concrete layout reference without importing the asset. An additional pair-link animation is referenced by the guide but was not visually inspected; no frame-level claim is made about it.

**Current PCR instructions, older article.** [Run PCR in silico](https://help.benchling.com/hc/en-us/articles/9684267245325-Run-PCR-in-silico) currently documents choosing/linking primers, creating a PCR product, choosing output options and saving a new sequence. The page labels its update relatively as four years ago; it is not evidence of newly introduced behavior.

**Current assembly guidance.** [Combinatorial cloning and concatenation](https://help.benchling.com/hc/en-us/articles/39922419457165-How-to-use-the-combinatorial-cloning-and-concatenation-assembly-tools) documents per-fragment preferred primers. It checks whether they satisfy fragment and assembly constraints; unsuitable choices can trigger replacement designs with status warnings. Construct and primer views are separate review surfaces, and primers/fragments can be optional outputs. Reuse the explicit validation/status concept; our proposal below deliberately requires approval before replacing a user's selected oligo.

**Historical manual-design detail.** The [April 10, 2019 PCR article](https://www.benchling.com/blog/in-silico-pcr-primer-design-and-gene-amplification) describes manual endpoint adjustment on the sequence, choosing separate forward/reverse selections, editing overhang and 3′ position, and attaching reused or newly saved primers before product creation. It also describes sorting saved-primer search results by useful sequence attributes. Treat its drag controls and exact layout as historical examples, not verified current widgets.

**Historical editing/junction detail.** The [March 16, 2014 Assembly Wizard announcement](https://www.benchling.com/blog/assembly-wizard) describes editing annealing/homology lengths and changing or inserting bases at primer-covered junctions, with corresponding oligo updates and undoable versions. The [December 16, 2014 Golden Gate announcement](https://www.benchling.com/blog/golden-gate-assembly-wizard) similarly describes point edits, labeled parent mismatches, export and finalization. Its [mutagenesis screenshot](https://images.ctfassets.net/kzeezny59h5p/J8OBGLXkyu0KNAU8Js5zq/49304f8f8f4f0767f1ff732d9c44ba2a/2015-04-screenshot-2014-12-16-15-00-16-2.png), visually inspected, places opposite oligo orientations beside the two-strand sequence and coordinate scale. The screenshot alone does not prove edit gestures or recalculation semantics; those come from the article text. Neither historical article establishes a current dedicated inverse-PCR wizard or its physical chemistry.

**Failure recovery.** Benchling's [primer-wizard troubleshooting article](https://help.benchling.com/hc/en-us/articles/14383972022925-Why-can-t-I-generate-Primers-using-the-Benchling-Primer-Wizard-for-certain-genes), updated August 19, 2024, recommends narrowing the included sequence and checking amplicon bounds when larger-genome designs fail. The interaction lesson is actionable parameter recovery, not silently relaxing filters or suggesting that a small selected-reference search proves global specificity.

**Unresolved evidence.** Searches of official Benchling help/blog sources did not locate a dedicated current inverse-PCR mutagenesis procedure. This is a research gap, not a claim that Benchling lacks that capability. Mutation and junction-editing interactions above are sourced; a claimed Benchling-specific back-to-back primer, phosphorylation, DpnI, host-repair or circularization sequence would be inference and is not adopted here. Benchling's [2022 protein-analysis teaching article](https://help.benchling.com/hc/en-us/articles/9684194939533-Protein-Analysis) documents choosing an amino acid and changing its codon; it does not establish a corresponding mutagenic primer/physical preparation workflow.

## Current repository: reuse what already exists

This inventory is source inspection, not a runtime acceptance claim. Current work may be newer than the published GUI.

- [Selection toolbar](../crates/web/src/viewer/sequence/selection_toolbar.rs) and [App wiring](../crates/web/src/app/mod.rs) provide a contiguous DNA selection → Make primer seed. The seed is reference-oriented and does not offer an explicit forward/reverse choice at that entry point.
- [Primer panel](../crates/web/src/primers/primers.rs) already separates annealing domains from 5′ tails, analyzes oligos, offers anchored pair selection, saves annotations and sends kept pairs into PCR. Its saved-primer handling now uses [the shared strict resolver](../crates/core/src/cloning/pcr/reuse.rs).
- [Shared primer picker](../crates/web/src/primers/primer_picker.rs) already supports cross-document reuse with actual document/event/revision binding. It is wired into the ordinary PCR controls and preparation rows in [cloning methods](../crates/web/src/cloning/preparation/cloning_methods.rs). Do not rebuild a second saved-primer mechanism or report this connection as missing.
- [Overlap planning](../crates/core/src/cloning/overlap/primer_design.rs) and the methods panel provide source-derived homology suggestions, an explicit apply action and subsequent preparation/verification. Suggested sequences are visible, but an interactive sequence-centered junction/domain editor is not supplied by that report alone.
- [Mutagenesis panel](../crates/web/src/cloning/methods/mutagenesis.rs) exposes declared-mismatch PCR and inverse-PCR replacement. The mismatch form uses manual domain/tail fields. The inverse form asks for source, edit boundaries, inserted sequence, domain lengths and tail split; the active document is the separately supplied intended circular target. It outputs an inspectable candidate and explicit PCR/phosphorylation/circular-ligation steps.
- [Core mutagenesis](../crates/core/src/cloning/pcr/mutagenesis.rs), native plan verification and [junction inspection](../crates/core/src/cloning/assembly/junctions.rs) already distinguish conditional mismatch rules, actual source mapping, unsupported alternatives and local frame/CDS evidence. Direct reference editing does not automatically rewrite a mutagenesis recipe or its oligos.
- [Oligo exchange](../crates/core/src/formats/oligos.rs) and [the import/export view](../crates/web/src/io/oligo_import.rs) already have order-table infrastructure, source preservation and spreadsheet-safe CSV/TSV. That view exports imported collections; the primer-design and inverse-PCR result views currently emphasize JSON rather than a direct selected-design order-sheet action.

## Concrete UI connections to implement

The following are our design recommendations inferred from the research and repository review. They are not claims about uninspected Benchling behavior.

| Connection | Concrete change | Scientific boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Selection → manual primer | Offer Forward / Reverse next to Make primer, and seed a common single/pair editor with the selected coordinates and explicit 5′→3′ orientation. Preserve current-selection feedback rather than requiring coordinate retyping. | Reverse oligo sequence comes from the selected strand orientation; the displayed reference selection remains clearly identified. A caret or unresolved multipart feature cannot silently become an exact annealing domain. |
| Manual, saved and suggested choices | Use the same editor in PCR and preparation rows, with explicit ways to enter bases, choose a saved oligo or accept a proposal. Keep current shared pickers. Add a visible per-slot summary and an intentional pair association. | Loading identity is not binding proof. Never replace an incompatible saved primer automatically; show the reason and offer a separately labeled suggestion. |
| Annealing versus introduced sequence | Render one ordered oligo with visibly distinct, editable tail/domain sections and clear 5′/3′ endpoints, while keeping separate model fields. Show expected changes before applying domain-boundary edits. | Tails are not initially annealed template bases. Preserve IUPAC and intentional mismatches; never label contiguous complementarity screens as complete folding/ΔG calculations. |
| Mutagenesis input reuse | Add the shared picker to mismatch-PCR forward/reverse slots, followed by editable domains/tails and a source-aligned mismatch display. Provide selection-to-edit-boundaries as well as numeric entry. | Changing the source/event, domain split, mismatch allowance or exact-3′ policy invalidates old products. Stored annotations do not grant amplification authority. |
| Edit intent → inverse PCR | Let users select source bases and declare Replace / Insert / Delete. Freeze an expected design from those source coordinates and explicit edits; show before/after DNA and optional codon context before choosing preparation. Keep independently imported targets as an optional route. | Expected DNA must come from frozen edit intent, never from copying the PCR/ligation simulator's answer. A reference edit alone is not synthesis, repair or a physical product. |
| Editable primer/junction draft | Select a junction or primer to edit homology, domains and intentional changes in one persistent draft. Show affected bases/oligos and keep undo/redo linked to that draft, followed by fresh preparation and target checks. | An edit covered by multiple oligos must show and update each affected oligo explicitly. If representation is unsupported or ambiguous, retain the draft and diagnostics rather than inventing a valid preparation. |
| Contextual errors | Put failures beside their source row/domain/junction. Offer concrete recovery: adjust the selected interval, length/GC/Tm filter, mismatch rule or product choice. Keep the user's source selection and manual oligos when a design fails. | Do not silently widen mismatch tolerances, discard competing products or relax a 3′ rule to get a green result. Bound work and explain incomplete enumeration. |
| Order and protocol output | Add selected-pair/all-required-oligos CSV/TSV actions to PCR, overlap and inverse-PCR review, using existing oligo export machinery. Show name, full ordered sequence, domain/tail distinction and declared modifications; retain JSON and HTML/PDF recipes. | Do not fabricate supplier stock IDs, ordering quantities, phosphorylation or reagent conditions. A proposed modification is explicit, not implied by the sequence or target match. |

A simple named document collection and bound primer annotations are sufficient. None of these connections needs projects, registries, reagent inventory, ELN or scheduling.

## Suggested implementation order and eventual review flows

First connect selection direction, the shared pair editor and saved-oligo choices to ordinary PCR and mismatch PCR. Then connect frozen edit intent to the existing inverse-PCR compiler and add the shared oligo order sheet. Finally make junction/domain editing and its linked undo/redo sequence-centered. This extends [the cloning interaction design](CLONING_INTERACTION_DESIGN.md); it does not replace its source/intent/verification separation.

When implementation is complete and browser review is authorized, walk through these concrete flows rather than adding more isolated parameter panels:

1. Select a reverse-strand binding region, create a named reverse primer with a tail, save it, reuse it from another document and inspect all actual PCR alternatives. Names and matching annotations must not choose the result.
2. Choose a saved primer that cannot amplify the selected region. Keep its literal sequence visible, show the mismatch/reason and offer a proposed replacement only through explicit acceptance.
3. Declare a substitution and an origin-spanning deletion on a circular source, inspect frozen intended DNA and selected preparation, edit one domain/tail and undo it. A stale product/check must never survive that edit.
4. Deliberately supply a wrong independent target or an unsupported overlapping-primer geometry. Keep the intended change, oligos and failure evidence available; neither a plausible local codon nor a manually edited preview can turn it into a verified clone.
5. Export the exact chosen ordered oligos and protocol. Confirm tails and explicit chemistry declarations remain visible and that original source documents and their history are preserved.

No external Primer3 process or new advanced folding engine is required to complete these interaction paths. Existing heuristic/Tm limitations should remain visible while the everyday cloning flow becomes direct.
